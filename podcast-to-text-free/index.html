Podcast to text: 4 free and cheap ways to transcribe a podcast episode

Before you transcribe anything, check whether a transcript already exists. Then pick the cheapest method that is accurate enough.

1. Look for an existing transcript (free)

Many shows publish transcripts in their RSS feed using the Podcasting 2.0 <podcast:transcript> tag, or on their website. Apple Podcasts and Spotify display auto-generated transcripts in their apps for many episodes, but don't offer an export. See how to download podcast transcripts from RSS.

2. Whisper on your own computer (free, slower)

pip install -U openai-whisper
whisper episode.mp3 --model small --language en --output_format txt

small is a good balance on a laptop; medium/large are more accurate but much slower without a GPU. Convert first if the file is huge: ffmpeg -i ep.mp3 -ac 1 -ar 16000 ep.wav.

3. Hosted Whisper by the minute

Services running Whisper large-v3 (for example Groq's API) transcribe an hour in well under a minute for a few cents. You need an API key and a little scripting.

4. From a link, no setup

The Podcast Transcriber on Apify takes an RSS feed, Apple Podcasts link or direct MP3 URL and returns TXT, SRT, VTT and JSON with word timestamps. It runs with or without your own Groq key and is billed per audio minute; Apify's free plan credit is enough to try it on a few short episodes.

Accuracy tips

  • Set the language explicitly instead of auto-detect for non-English shows.
  • Expect errors on names, jargon and crosstalk; search the transcript for proper nouns before publishing.
  • Only transcribe content you have the rights to use for your purpose.
